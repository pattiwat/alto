# Which Grey-Box Technique for the Floor 8 Surrogate

> **What this is.** The answer to a follow-up question asked on 8 September 2026: *in option A2 of
> [rl-sac-feasibility.md](rl-sac-feasibility.md) — a grey-box surrogate with online RL on top —
> which grey-box technique should the surrogate use?*
>
> **Companion documents.** [grey-box-surrogate.md](grey-box-surrogate.md) is the block-by-block
> specification this memo revises; [rl-sac-feasibility.md](rl-sac-feasibility.md) is the decision
> that a surrogate is needed at all; [control-gap-method.md](control-gap-method.md) supplies every
> measured air-side coefficient. Nothing below is implemented in `src/`. The zone-block numbers come
> from three exploratory scripts run against the raw CSV today; they are quoted so the technique
> choice is evidence-based, and **they must be regenerated by `src/rl/identify.py` before any of them
> appears in the report.**
>
> **Bottom line up front.** Use a *block-oriented, physics-structured grey-box*: quasi-steady static
> maps for the air-side plant (already measured — that part of the spec stands), and for the zones a
> **two-node lumped-capacitance model, zone air coupled to an internal mass node**, identified by
> **two-stage least squares** — the air-to-mass coupling from the unforced evening response after the
> fan stops, the supply-air term by NNLS on daytime steps with stage 1 fixed — then refined on
> multi-step simulation error, with a **day-block bootstrap ensemble** supplying the domain-
> randomisation distribution. The spec's single-node-to-outdoor form is mis-specified on this floor:
> the outdoor coupling fits to zero and the fit is three times worse. The textbook alternative, a full
> state-space model by prediction-error method with a Kalman filter, is the upgrade path if the
> two-stage fit fails the open-loop gate; it is not needed first.

---

## 1. What "grey-box technique" has to decide

A grey-box surrogate is a set of choices, not one method. Four of them matter here:

| Choice | Question | Where it is decided |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | Which physical states and couplings exist, per block | §3, §4.1 |
| **Identification objective** | One-step equation error, multi-step output error, or likelihood with a filter | §4.2 |
| **Uncertainty** | How parameter uncertainty is represented and fed to the agent | §4.3 |
| **Validation** | What falsifies the model before an agent is trained on it | §5.3 |

The air-side blocks (fan, duct, boxes, SAT loop) are quasi-steady static maps by necessity — the
record is 15-minute averages, below Nyquist for every inner loop (grey-box §0) — and their
coefficients are already measured. The technique question is therefore almost entirely about the
**zone thermal block**, which is the only dynamic part of the surrogate and the only part the
existing spec got structurally wrong.

## 2. Evidence from the CSV

### 2.1 The zones are coupled to building mass, not to outdoor air

Floor-mean zone temperature by hour of day, all days:

| Time | Zone mean | Outdoor | What is happening |
|---|---|---|---|
| 17:00 | 23.4 °C | 32.0 °C | Fan on, zones held cold |
| 18:00 | 24.0 °C | 30.9 °C | Fan stops |
| 19:00 | 24.5 °C | 29.9 °C | Re-equilibrating |
| 21:00 → 05:00 | **24.7 °C, flat** | 29.0 → 27.9 °C | **Plateau 4 K below outdoor, all night** |
| 08:00 | 23.4 °C | 29.0 °C | Pulled down within two hours of start |

A zone coupled to outdoor air keeps warming all night. These stop within about three hours and hold.
Only a large internal mass — slab, structure, the conditioned floors above and below — can hold them
there. It is not equipment: the 14 fan coil units are off on **100%** of night and weekend steps;
AHU-1 runs on 2% of weekend steps; AHU-2 on 2% of night steps and 8% of weekend steps.

### 2.2 The mass node is a real, slow state

Per-night plateau, floor mean over 21:00–05:00, 97 nights:

| Statistic | Value |
|---|---|
| Mean, std | 24.75 °C, 0.54 K |
| Range | 23.6 → 26.1 °C |
| Correlation with night outdoor temperature | 0.34 |
| Lag-1 autocorrelation | 0.48 |
| Weekly cycle | Mon-night 24.9 → Fri-night 24.2; Sat-night 25.05; Sun-night 25.6 |
| Weekend drift, Friday night → Sunday night | **+1.4 K** |

The mass cools about 0.15 K per weekday under daytime cooling and warms about 0.7 K per uncooled
day. Within one 44-step occupied episode it moves by under 0.3 K, so it can be held constant per
episode. Over a multi-day rollout it needs a slow first-order law.

### 2.3 Two-stage identification works; the spec's one-node form does not

Prototype on 50 boxes. Two sensors excluded as garbage (`vav_8_1_9` day mean 8.4 °C, `vav_8_1_6`
19.7 °C); the pinned-damper rogue `vav_8_1_28` fails stage 1 and is pooled.

| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| **Stage 1** — evening free response, fan off 18:00–23:00: `ΔT = k·(T_m − T)` | k > 0 on **50 of 50** boxes; **τ = Δt/k median 2.5 h, IQR 2.1–2.9 h**; R² 0.16 median (0.3–0.6 on AHU-2, 0.02–0.15 on AHU-1, whose room sensors are noisier) |
| **Stage 2** — daytime NNLS with k fixed: `ΔT − k·(T_m − T) = a·V̇·(T_sa − T) + g·(T_oa − T) + c` | a > 0 on **96%** of boxes; **median R² 0.185**; outdoor coupling g fits to exactly zero on 40 of 50 boxes and is negligible on the rest |
| Spec's form on the same steps: `ΔT = a·V̇·(T_sa − T) + g·(T_oa − T) + c` | **median R² 0.056** — one third of the two-stage fit; g ≈ 0 again |
| Joint daytime fit with k free | τ comes out **7.1 h**, three times the free-response value, because k and a are collinear when both are driven by T. This is why stage 1 must come from the fan-off window |

The spec's `γ_i·(T_oa − T_i)` term is the wrong coupling. Its fit pushes the missing mass term into
the intercept `ε_i`, which does not transfer between days with different plateaus and drifts in any
multi-step rollout — exactly the failure the spec's own §8.2 open-loop test would catch, after the
block had been built.

### 2.4 The air-side effects are measured, not argued

From the episode check in rl-sac-feasibility §2.3: over 8 AHU-1 pressure-cut episodes the median
treated/control ratios were 0.956 (fan Hz), **0.939 (delivered airflow)**, 0.917 (fan kW). β, the
fan curve and the SAT bias are in `report/` already.

> **Amended 10 Sep 2026 — see [block-c-derivation/README.md](block-c-derivation/README.md) §9.**
> The airflow ratio is real, but this section used to read it as confirming *the saturation
> mechanism*, and it does not. Over the same 8 episodes the mean damper position **fell** (45.51 vs
> 47.16) and **no box was saturated in either window** — if boxes had been running out of authority,
> both would have gone the other way. The floor was 0.89 K cooler during the cuts, so the zone loops
> were closing dampers on falling demand. What the episodes *do* confirm is Block C's allocation: the
> share model places the observed total across rooms to within 5.3% of total flow, out of sample.

### 2.5 Identifiability of the supply-air term

Median coefficient of variation of box airflow during on-hours is 0.32. Eight AHU-1 boxes — the ones
parked at a 27 °C setpoint (`vav_8_1_{1,2,3,4,5,10,11,14}`) — have CV below 0.1 and cannot identify
`a` alone. Pool them into one shared fit, as the spec's §5.3 already proposes.

## 3. Options, with trade-offs

### 3.1 Zone thermal block structure

| | Technique | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Z1 | **One-node RC to outdoor, one-step NNLS** — grey-box-surrogate §5 | Simplest; four parameters per zone; linear in parameters | Mis-specified here: outdoor coupling fits to zero, R² 0.056, mass effect lands in the intercept and drifts across days | **Replace** |
| **Z2** | **Two-node air + internal mass; mass observed as the night plateau; two-stage least squares** | Matches the data (τ 2.5 h; R² three times Z1); stage 1 has no controller in the loop, so no endogeneity; one extra exogenous input per day; ~100 lines, prototype exists | Mass constant per episode only; multi-day rollouts need a slow law; AHU-1 sensor noise limits per-zone precision | **Use** |
| Z3 | **Full 2R2C / 3R2C state-space, prediction-error method with Kalman filter** (the CTSM approach; Bacher & Madsen 2011) | The textbook building grey-box; mass as a latent state; parameter covariances for free; robust to measurement noise | 3–5 h more; likelihood optimisation with a filter in the loop; with the mass node directly observed it adds little | **Upgrade path** if Z2 fails the open-loop gate |
| Z4 | ARX / ARMAX per zone | Fast; standard tooling | No physical constraints; endogeneity uncorrected; parameters not interpretable, so no physically meaningful DR ranges; unreliable at counterfactual airflow | No |
| Z5 | Physics + learned residual (GP or small NN on Z2 residuals) | Captures unmodelled effects | The residual learns the operator's confound and the zone controllers; unauditable on ~50 training days | No |
| Z6 | Modelica / EnergyPlus calibrated model | Full physics | No geometry, envelope or internal-gain data; days of work; still needs calibration to this record | No |
| Z7 | Koopman / DMDc linear latent model | Modern; linear-MPC-friendly | Same extrapolation problem as Z4 with more machinery | No |

### 3.2 Identification objective

| | Objective | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I1 | **Equation error** — one-step OLS / NNLS | Closed form; fast; what the spec does | Biased by controller endogeneity (spec §5.4); optimises the wrong thing for an environment used in 44-step rollouts | Initialisation only |
| **I2** | **Output error** — minimise 8-step simulation error, bounded least squares, initialised from I1 | Fits what the environment is used for; partially corrects the endogeneity bias; `scipy.optimize.least_squares` with bounds ≥ 0 | Non-convex; needs the I1 start; slower | **Use** |
| I3 | Prediction-error method with Kalman filter | Handles noise formally; consistent estimates | Part of Z3; heavier | With Z3 only |

### 3.3 Parameter uncertainty and domain randomisation

| | Method | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| U1 | Hand-set DR intervals — spec §8.2 | Simple; matches the tornado axes | Zone-block ranges would be guesses | Keep for β, fan model, SAT bias, ignore-*I* |
| **U2** | **Day-block bootstrap ensemble** — K parameter sets, one sampled per episode | Grounded in data; reuses `masks.block_bootstrap_indices` with days as blocks; the ensemble spread *is* the reported uncertainty | K refits; K ≈ 20 suffices | **Use for the zone block** |
| U3 | Bayesian posterior (MCMC) | Principled | Overkill; same information as U2 at ten times the cost | No |

### 3.4 Air-side blocks

Keep the spec's technique unchanged. Quasi-steady static maps are forced by the sampling rate, and
the coefficients are measured: β from `report/elasticities.csv`, the fan curve from
`report/fan_models.csv`, the SAT bias from control-gap §3. No alternative technique beats a measured
static map here, and the one caveat — the fan-model table now differs from the numbers quoted in the
memos (rl-sac-feasibility §7.1) — is a bookkeeping fix, not a technique change.

> **Amended 10 Sep 2026.** This section used to end "Block C stays closed-form with the two damper
> regimes." It no longer does. Block C is still closed-form and still a static map — the technique
> claim above is unaffected — but the two regimes are gone, replaced by a **share**: each room gets
> `a_i·d_i / Σ_j a_j·d_j` of a total set by aggregate damper opening and duct static. See
> [grey-box-surrogate.md](grey-box-surrogate.md) §4.2 and
> [block-c-derivation/README.md](block-c-derivation/README.md).
>
> Two things this changes for the technique claim. Block C is now **fitted** — one weight per room —
> which moves it out of the "measured static map, no parameters of its own" category this section
> places it in. And static pressure **cancels from the room allocation entirely**, so the pressure
> exponent that had to be imposed under the earlier per-box valve law is confined to the total, which
> is the weak half of the block (R² 0.42 / 0.64).

## 4. The recommended technique, in full

### 4.1 Zone block equations

Per zone *i*, Δt = 0.25 h:

```
T_i(t+1) − T_i(t) = k_i · (T_m,i − T_i(t))                     air ↔ internal mass
                  + a_i · V̇_i(t) · (T_sa(t) − T_i(t))           supply air
                  + g_i · (T_oa(t) − T_i(t))                    outdoor  (expected ≈ 0)
                  + d_i · occ(t) + c_i                          gains
```

with `k_i, a_i, g_i ≥ 0`. Implied air time constant `τ_i = Δt / (k_i + a_i·V̇_i + g_i)`, expected
2–3 h at zero flow.

**`T_m,i` for an episode** = mean of zone *i*'s own reading over 21:00–05:00 of the night that ended
that morning, **replayed as an exogenous input** alongside weather and occupancy. Fallback to the
floor mean when the zone's night data is missing. This is the structural choice that makes Z2 cheap:
the slow state is measured once a day, so it never has to be estimated.

**For multi-day rollouts only:**

```
T_m(t+1) = T_m(t) + (Δt / τ_m) · (T_i(t) − T_m(t))
```

with `τ_m` fitted from the weekend sawtooth (≈ 0.7 K per uncooled day toward the air).

### 4.2 Identification procedure

1. Mask via `masks.analysis_mask` and `masks.mask_room_temp_sentinels`; drop `vav_8_1_9` and
   `vav_8_1_6` as sensors; **training window only** (18 May → 29 Jul).
2. **Stage 1**, per zone: fan off, 18:00–23:00, regress `ΔT` on `(T_m − T)` through the origin.
   Reject a zone if `k ≤ 0` or τ falls outside 0.5–8 h, and pool it.
3. **Stage 2**, per zone: fan on, weekdays 08:00–17:00, NNLS on `[a, g]` with intercept, `k` fixed
   from stage 1. Pool the eight parked zones into one shared fit.
4. **Refine** all zone parameters jointly by 8-step output error with bounds ≥ 0, starting from
   steps 2–3.
5. **Bootstrap**: resample training days with replacement, repeat steps 2–4, K = 20. Store the
   ensemble as `report/zone_rc_ensemble.csv`; the point estimate as `report/zone_rc.csv`.

### 4.3 How the ensemble reaches the agent

At each episode reset, sample one parameter set from the K, together with the spec's other DR axes
(β from its CI, one of the three fan models, SAT bias on or off, ignore-*I*). The tornado chart and
the training distribution remain the same object, as the spec intends — the zone axis is now
data-derived rather than guessed.

### 5.2 Corrections to `grey-box-surrogate.md`

- **§5.1–5.2**: the outdoor coupling `γ_i·(T_oa − T_i)` is the wrong structure for this floor.
  Replace with the two-node form; `T_oa` stays as a minor term expected to fit to zero.
- **§5.3 step 3**: NNLS per zone becomes the two-stage procedure of §4.2.
- **§5.4**: the endogeneity mitigations are superseded — stage 1 has no controller in the loop, and
  the output-error refinement handles the rest. The instrumenting-by-duct-pressure upgrade is no
  longer needed.
- **§5.5**: add the night plateau (24.7 °C) and the evening settling (τ ≈ 2.5 h) to what the fit must
  reproduce. Both are sharper tests than the −2.6 K undercooling, because neither involves the
  controller.
- **§8.1**: the zone row gains `k_i`; provenance "fitted, stage 1, free response".
- **§8.2**: the zone-block DR axis becomes the bootstrap ensemble.

### 5.3 Validation gates, before any agent training

1. **Open loop.** 8-step rollout on the test month beats persistence on zone temperature RMSE for
   the floor mean and for at least 80% of active zones.
2. **Free response.** Switching the fan off in simulation settles every active zone to its `T_m`
   within 3τ.
3. **Undercooling.** The rollout reproduces the −2.6 K mean AHU-1 offset below setpoint (spec §5.5).
4. **Air side.** Elasticity recovery at β ± CI and the specific-fan-power consistency test, as
   already specified.
5. **If gate 1 fails, escalate to Z3.** Do not tune Z2 by hand until it passes.

## 6. What this does and does not establish

**Established today, from the CSV:**

1. The zones equilibrate overnight to an internal mass at ≈ 24.7 °C, with τ ≈ 2.5 h, and that mass
   is a slow state with a weekly cycle. Not equipment: FCUs are off all night and all weekend.
2. A two-node model with the mass observed fits daytime zone dynamics three times better than the
   spec's one-node-to-outdoor form, and its outdoor coupling is zero on most zones.
3. Fitting the air-to-mass coupling from the fan-off window removes the controller from the
   identification; fitting it jointly with daytime data biases τ threefold.

**Not established, and stated as such:**

1. The physical identity of the mass node (slab vs adjacent floors) — irrelevant to a single-day
   episode, since the surrogate replays it, but it decides whether `τ_m` is a property of this floor.
2. Per-zone precision on AHU-1, where the room sensors are noisy (stage-1 R² 0.02–0.15). Pooled or
   shrunk estimates may be needed there; the bootstrap will show which zones are unstable.
3. Whether the one-step R² of ≈ 0.19 translates into a rollout that beats persistence. That is what
   gate 1 is for, and it is the only number that matters for the environment.

## 7. Assumptions

- The night plateau is treated as a boundary temperature for this floor whatever its source.
- Airflow units remain unresolved; `a_i` absorbs them, and counterfactual airflow enters only as a
  ratio from Block C.
- The rule-based Task B is built first. This memo does not change the phase ordering in
  rl-sac-feasibility §4.
